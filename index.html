The problem

Phlebectomy is common, effective and still operator intensive.

Ambulatory phlebectomy removes diseased superficial veins through small incisions. It is one of the most frequently performed treatments for symptomatic varicose veins, and it works.

The weak point is bleeding control. Today a surgeon hooks and extracts the vein while an assistant applies pressure or handles cautery. That adds staff cost, extends procedure time, and introduces variability in hemostasis from case to case.

Chronic venous disease is also progressive. Left untreated it advances from visible veins to swelling, skin changes and ulceration, which is why early intervention volume keeps growing.

The device

A battery operated bipolar cautery clamp system, built for phlebectomy.

One reusable handle. Disposable tips sized to the vein. Grasp the tissue, deliver precise bipolar coagulation, move on.

Reusable handle

Ergonomic, cordless, single handed

A battery operated, rechargeable handle designed for one handed use during phlebectomy. No generator, no foot pedal, no cart.

Interchangeable tips

Disposable end effectors sized to vein diameter

Each tip grasps tissue and delivers targeted bipolar coagulation. Sizing to the vessel keeps energy where it belongs and creates a recurring revenue line per procedure.

Consistent hemostasis

Fewer hands, less time, repeatable results

By combining grasp and seal in one instrument, the system removes the need for a surgical assistant and reduces overall procedure time.

Business model

Capital equipment at the front, consumables on every case.

Revenue

Handle plus disposable tips

A reusable handle sold to the practice or facility, and a sized disposable tip consumed on every phlebectomy. Recurring revenue scales with procedure volume.
